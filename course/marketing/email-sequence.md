## Email 2: A useful lesson (day 2)
**Subject:** The 20-second fix for robotic-sounding AI writing
**Preview:** Use your own words as the sample.

Hi [First name],

If AI writing sounds fake to you, here's why: it's never read anything you've written.

The fix:
1. Paste 2–3 short things you've written (only things you're comfortable sharing).
2. Ask: "Describe my writing style in 5 bullets: sentence length, tone, vocabulary, habits, things I avoid."
3. Save the answer as "My voice."
4. Next time, say: "Write [task] in this style. Avoid clichés. Don't invent facts."

Then read the result out loud. If you wouldn't say it, change it.

That's one of the first things I teach in AI for Everyday Work, my beginner course. More on that soon.

Antonio

## Email 3: The honest side (day 4)
**Subject:** Why I don't trust AI (and what I do instead)
**Preview:** Confident isn't the same as correct.

Hi [First name],

In my internship, part of my job is testing where AI is reliable for research and where it isn't. In one project I compared AI-generated forecasts to what actually happened across dozens of companies. In that sample, the forecasts leaned optimistic and weren't reliably better than simple alternatives. It always sounded confident. That's the problem with AI: confidence isn't evidence.

So I use a simple three-step habit:
1. **Flag** every number, name, date, quote and source.
2. **Verify** each one against a reliable source (not by asking the AI "are you sure?").
3. **Decide:** if you can't verify it, cut it.

Try it on the next AI answer you get. Check three facts and see how many hold up.

Antonio
