# Module 2: Emails in Minutes (21 min)

*Outcome 2: draft and polish professional emails in a fraction of the time.*

**Module rules to repeat on camera:** never paste private client, customer, classmate or medical information; check every date, name and number; you're responsible for what you send.

---

## Lesson 2.1: From bullet points to a finished email (5 min)

### Script

[SCREEN: You on camera, then a blank email draft with the cursor blinking.]

Staring at a blank email is one of the most common time-wasters. You know what you want to say. You just don't know how to say it politely and professionally.

Here's the fix. Don't ask AI to write the email from nothing. Give it your bullet points, and let it turn them into a draft.

[SCREEN: Open a notes app. Type rough bullets.]

Say I need to ask my manager to move a meeting. My bullets: I have a conflict Thursday at 2. Can we do Friday morning or Monday? Agenda is the same. Sorry for the change.

[SCREEN: Paste into the prompt template. Read it aloud as you type.]

Here's the prompt. I tell it who I'm writing to and my relationship, what I want, my key points, the tone and the length. And I add: don't invent any details.

[SCREEN: Show the result. Highlight the subject line.]

There's a subject line, a short greeting, the request and clear options. It took about twenty seconds.

[SCREEN: Edit the email in the doc. Change a phrase, add the real time.]

Now I read it. Is everything true? Are the times right? Does it sound like something I'd say? I change one phrase, and it's ready.

Notice what I did. I supplied the facts. The AI supplied the structure and wording. That's the right split.

[SCREEN: On camera.]

One more habit: always give the AI the goal. Do you want a yes, information, an apology accepted, a decision? Emails with one clear ask get answered faster.

**Good to know:** don't paste emails that contain private information about other people. Remove names and details you don't need, or replace them with placeholders like [CLIENT].

### Worked example
**Scenario:** Maya, a coordinator, needs to reschedule a Thursday 2 p.m. meeting with her manager, Chris.

**Bullets:** conflict Thursday 2 p.m.; propose Friday 10 a.m. or Monday 9 a.m.; same agenda; short apology.

**Result (sample):**
> Subject: Request to reschedule Thursday's 2 p.m. meeting
>
> Hi Chris,
>
> I have a conflict Thursday at 2 p.m. and need to move our meeting. Would Friday at 10 a.m. or Monday at 9 a.m. work for you? The agenda would stay the same.
>
> Sorry for the change, and thanks for your flexibility.
>
> Best,
> Maya

### Prompt template
```
Write an email from me ([your role]) to [recipient and relationship].
Goal: [what I want them to do or know].
Key points:
- [point 1]
- [point 2]
- [point 3]
Tone: [friendly / professional / firm but polite].
Length: under [X] words. Include a subject line.
Don't invent any details. Use [PLACEHOLDER] for anything you don't know.
```

### Do it in 10 minutes
Pick an email you need to send this week. Write three bullets, use the template, edit the draft, and send it or save it.

### Quiz
1. **What should you give AI when drafting an email?** Your facts and bullets, the goal, tone and length.
2. **Why say "don't invent details"?** It stops the AI from adding false specifics.
3. **What should you do before sending?** Read it, check facts and dates, and make sure it sounds like you.

## Lesson 2.2: Matching your voice and the situation (5 min)

### Script

[SCREEN: You on camera. Then three email drafts side by side: to a professor, a friend-turned-teammate, an unhappy customer.]

The same message can need totally different wording depending on who's reading. What you'd send a professor isn't what you'd send a teammate, and definitely isn't what you'd send someone you need to push back on.

AI is very good at adjusting tone. You just need to tell it what you want.

[SCREEN: Type a base email, then show tone instructions.]

Start with a draft, yours or one AI wrote. Then ask it to rewrite for the situation. Here are some tone words that work: warm, direct, confident but polite, formal, casual, apologetic without over-apologizing, firm but respectful.

[SCREEN: Run one draft through "more direct" and "warmer." Show both outputs.]

See how the direct version cuts the extra words, and the warm one adds a friendlier opening. Same content, different feel.

[SCREEN: Open your saved "My voice" note from Lesson 1.3.]

Now combine this with your voice note from Module One. "Rewrite in my style, and make it a bit more formal since it's going to a professor."

[SCREEN: Show a "Tone cheat sheet" slide: Professor = respectful, brief, clear ask. Manager = concise, outcome first. Recruiter = polite, specific, professional. Peer = casual, friendly.]

Here are quick tone guides. Professors: respectful, brief, a clear ask. Managers: get to the point, outcome first. Recruiters: polite, specific, professional. Peers: casual and friendly.

[SCREEN: On camera.]

One warning. Don't let AI make you sound like someone you're not. If an email is way more formal or enthusiastic than you normally are, people notice. Tone it down until it feels like you on a good day.

### Worked example
**Scenario:** Jordan wants to ask a professor for a recommendation letter.

Base draft (Jordan's bullets): took her class last fall, got an A-, applying to two grad programs, deadline in 4 weeks.

Prompt:
```
Rewrite this email for a professor I respect but don't know well. Tone: respectful, warm, brief. Make the ask clear: would she be willing to write a strong recommendation letter? Mention the deadline. Use my style: short sentences, sincere, no exclamation points. Don't invent details.
```
Jordan then removes one flattering line that doesn't sound like them.

### Prompt templates
```
Rewrite this email for [recipient]. Tone: [tone]. Keep the meaning the same and the length under [X] words.

Email: [paste]
```
```
Here are two versions of the same email with different tones. Explain in 2 sentences the difference, then tell me which fits [situation] better.
```
```
Rewrite this in my voice: [voice note]. Make it slightly more [formal/casual] because it's going to [who].
```

### Do it in 10 minutes
Take one email draft and generate three versions: more direct, warmer and more formal. Choose the closest to how you'd talk, and note the phrases you like.

### Quiz
1. **What can you tell AI to change without changing the message?** The tone.
2. **Name two tone words from the lesson.** Any: warm, direct, formal, casual, firm but respectful.
3. **What's a red flag in an AI-tone edit?** It sounds much more formal or enthusiastic than you actually are.

## Lesson 2.3: Replies, long threads and tricky emails (6 min)

### Script

[SCREEN: You on camera. Then an inbox with a long email chain of 12 messages.]

Email isn't only writing new messages. A lot of the work is catching up on long threads, replying, following up and handling awkward situations. AI can help with all of those.

**Summarizing threads first.**

[SCREEN: Copy a sample thread (fictional, with names replaced by placeholders). Paste into the chat with the prompt.]

Here's a fake thread of twelve messages. Instead of reading every reply, I ask: "Summarize this thread in five bullets. List decisions made, open questions and who's responsible for what."

[SCREEN: Show the summary. Then scroll the original thread to verify one item.]

Handy. But spot-check it against the original. Summaries can miss things or mix up who said what.

**Now replies.**

[SCREEN: Paste one incoming email. Prompt: "Draft a reply that says yes to A, no to B and asks about C."]

Paste the message you got, then tell it what you decide. Yes to this, no to that, ask this question. Don't ask it to make the decision for you. You decide, it phrases.

**Follow-ups.**

[SCREEN: Show a follow-up prompt with "friendly, not pushy."]

For a follow-up after silence: "Polite follow-up, under 60 words, friendly, not pushy." Done.

**Tricky emails.**

[SCREEN: Show three scenario titles: declining a request, asking for a deadline extension, pushing back on a request.]

Declining, asking for more time, disagreeing. These are the emails we overthink. Give the AI the situation and your goal: "I need to say no to this request, keep the relationship positive and suggest an alternative."

[SCREEN: Show the output, then edit one line.]

Read it and ask, is this honest? Don't let it make up excuses. If the draft says "I have a prior commitment" and you don't, change it. AI will happily write a polite excuse that isn't true. Don't send that.

[SCREEN: On camera.]

And for anything emotional, legal, HR-related or high-stakes, use AI for a first draft at most, and have a real person you trust read it before you send.

### Worked example
**Scenario:** Maya's teammate asks her to take over a report on short notice. She can't say yes fully.

```
I received the email below. My decision: I can't take the whole report, but I can review the final numbers on Wednesday. Write a friendly, clear reply that says no to the full report, offers the Wednesday review, and asks who else might help. Under 90 words. Don't invent reasons or details.

Email: [paste]
```

### Prompt templates
**Summarize a thread**
```
Summarize this email thread in 5 bullets: decisions made, open questions, who owes what, and deadlines. If anything is unclear, list it under "Unclear." Thread: [paste, with names replaced by initials if needed]
```
**Reply with my decision**
```
Draft a reply to the email below. My decisions: [yes to X / no to Y / question about Z]. Tone: [tone]. Under [X] words. Don't add facts I haven't given you.

Email: [paste]
```
**Follow-up**
```
Write a polite follow-up to [name] about [topic]. I first emailed on [date]. Friendly, not pushy, under 60 words.
```
**Decline politely**
```
I need to decline [request] from [person]. Keep the relationship positive, be honest (no invented excuses), and offer [alternative or nothing]. Under 100 words.
```

### Do it in 10 minutes
Find a real (or sample) email thread with placeholders instead of private names. Summarize it and check two facts against the original. Then draft one reply using your decisions.

### Quiz
1. **Who makes the decision in a reply, you or the AI?** You.
2. **Why spot-check a thread summary?** It can miss things or get details wrong.
3. **What's a risk in a "polite decline" draft?** It may invent a false excuse, so edit for honesty.

## Lesson 2.4: Polish without over-editing (5 min)

### Script

[SCREEN: You on camera. Then an email you drafted, with typos and a rambling second paragraph.]

You wrote your own email, and it's decent. You just want it cleaner. AI is good at polishing, as long as you keep it on a short leash.

The danger is over-editing. If you say "improve this," it may rewrite your whole message and remove your personality. So be specific about the level of editing.

[SCREEN: Show three prompt options on screen: proofread only, tighten, rewrite.]

Level one: **Proofread only.** "Fix spelling, grammar and punctuation only. Don't change wording."

Level two: **Tighten.** "Shorten by about a third without losing any key points. Keep my tone."

Level three: **Rewrite.** Only when you want a new version.

[SCREEN: Run level one on the draft. Show the changes.]

Most of the time, level one or two is all you need.

[SCREEN: Run this prompt: "Show me what you changed as a list."]

Here's a good habit. Ask, "List what you changed." That keeps you in control, so you can see whether it changed the meaning.

[SCREEN: Use "Read like the recipient" prompt.]

Another handy move: "Read this as [the recipient] and tell me how it might come across, and whether anything is unclear or could be read the wrong way." It's a quick sanity check on tone before you send.

[SCREEN: Show a final checklist: names spelled right, dates correct, attachments mentioned, one clear ask, sounds like me.]

And a final checklist before you hit send. Names spelled right. Dates and numbers correct. Attachment attached. One clear ask. Sounds like me.

[SCREEN: On camera.]

Sometimes the best answer is: send it as it is. If your email is short, clear and polite, more editing won't help. Not everything needs AI.

### Worked example
**Scenario:** Jordan's draft to a landlord about a broken heater:

> hi, my heater has been broken for 3 days and its really cold. i emailed last week and nobody replied. can someone come fix it asap. thanks

Prompt at level two, plus a tone check:
```
Fix spelling and grammar, and make this slightly more professional while keeping it short and firm. Don't add any facts. Then list what you changed.
```
Result keeps the facts (3 days, previous email last week) and adds a subject line and greeting. Jordan confirms nothing new was invented.

### Prompt templates
```
Proofread only: fix spelling, grammar and punctuation. Don't change wording or tone. List what you changed.

Text: [paste]
```
```
Tighten this by about a third, keeping every key point and my tone. Show me the result and list anything you removed.

Text: [paste]
```
```
Read this email as [recipient]. How might it come across? Is anything unclear, too blunt, or easy to misread? Suggest up to 3 small changes, not a full rewrite.

Email: [paste]
```

### Do it in 10 minutes
Take a real email you wrote. Run "proofread only," then "how might this come across," and only accept changes you agree with.

### Quiz
1. **What's the least invasive editing level?** Proofread only.
2. **Why ask the AI to list what it changed?** So you can catch meaning changes and stay in control.
3. **When might you skip AI entirely?** When the email is already short, clear and polite.
