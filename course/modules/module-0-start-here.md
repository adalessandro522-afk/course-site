## Lesson 0.2: Set up your AI toolkit (5 min)

### Script

[SCREEN: Browser with four tabs open: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot (home pages, logged out).]

There are lots of AI assistants. The four you'll hear about most are ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot. They all work the same basic way: you type a message, called a prompt, and it types back. A prompt is just an instruction or question.

You don't need all four. Pick one to start. If you already have a Google account, Gemini is easy. If you use Microsoft Office at school or work, try Copilot. ChatGPT and Claude are both good general options. I'll use different ones throughout, and everything in this course works on any of them.

[SCREEN: Click "sign up" on one tool, show the account creation screen. Blur or skip the actual credentials.]

Make a free account. **Good to know:** the free versions are enough for this course. Paid plans usually add things like higher usage limits, newer models and extra features, and those change often, so I'll tell you when a lesson goes better with a paid one.

[SCREEN: Show the main chat screen. Circle the message box, the attach/upload button, the "new chat" button and the settings menu.]

Four things to find. The message box, where you type. The upload button, usually a paperclip or plus sign, for attaching a file. The new chat button. And settings. Interfaces change, so if yours looks different, look for the same four things.

New chat matters more than you'd think. AI remembers what's earlier in the same conversation. If you switch topics in the same chat, it can get confused or mix things up. Rule of thumb: new task, new chat.

[SCREEN: Open Settings, find the data/privacy section. Show the option about using your conversations to improve the model, if it exists.]

One more step, and I'll go deeper in module seven. Open your settings and find the privacy or data controls. Many tools let you choose whether your conversations can be used to train future versions of the AI. Look at that setting and decide on purpose.

[SCREEN: Type "Hi! In two sentences, explain what you can help me with as a beginner." Show the response.]

Now send a test message to make sure it works. There. You're set up. Next lesson, you'll get your first real win.

### Worked example
Show the same test message in two tools side by side. Point out the answers differ, and that's normal: the tools differ, and the same tool can give different answers each time.

### Prompt template
```
Hi! I'm a complete beginner. In two sentences, tell me what you can help me with, then give me three simple things I could ask you to try today.
```

### Do it in 10 minutes
1. Create an account on one assistant.
2. Find the message box, upload button, new chat button and settings.
3. Open the privacy settings and read what they say about your data.
4. Send the test prompt.

### Quiz
1. **What's a prompt?** The instruction or question you type to the AI.
2. **Why start a new chat for a new task?** The AI uses earlier messages as context and can get confused.
3. **Do you need a paid plan for this course?** No. The free versions are enough, and paid features are flagged.

## Lesson 0.3: Your first 10-minute win (4 min)

### Script

[SCREEN: You on camera.]

Let's get a quick win before we get into theory. Grab one of those slow tasks you wrote down, something small. A short email, a summary, a plan for your week.

[SCREEN: Open a new chat. Type the "before" prompt: "Write an email asking my professor for an extension."]

Here's what most beginners do. They type something short and vague, like "Write an email asking my professor for an extension."

[SCREEN: Show the output. Highlight generic phrases like "I hope this email finds you well" and the placeholder text.]

And you get something generic. It says "I hope this email finds you well," it invents details, and it sounds like nobody in particular. The AI doesn't know who you are or what's going on. So it guesses.

Now watch what happens when I give it real information.

[SCREEN: Type the "after" prompt slowly so it's readable. Then show the result.]

Same request, but I told it who I am, what happened, what I want and how long it should be. The email is shorter, more specific and much closer to something I'd send. I'll still read it and change a couple of things. But I'm editing, not starting from scratch.

That's the whole idea of this course. The AI isn't magic. It's a fast first-draft partner, and the quality of the draft depends on the information you give it, and on how carefully you review it.

[SCREEN: Show a checklist: "Did it invent anything? Does it sound like me? Is it accurate?"]

Before you send anything AI helped write, ask three things. Did it invent anything? Does it sound like me? Is every fact true? Fix what's not.

Now you try one. Do your own task, then come back for lesson one.

### Worked example
**Scenario:** Jordan, a junior, missed a lab report deadline because of a family emergency.

**Weak prompt:** "Write an email asking my professor for an extension."

**Better prompt:**
```
I'm a college junior emailing my professor, Dr. Patel, in my Organic Chemistry course. I missed Friday's lab report deadline because of a family emergency. I'd like to ask for a 3-day extension. Write a polite, honest email of about 100 words. Don't invent any details beyond what I've told you, and leave the date of the emergency as [DATE].
```
**What to point out:** the second version has who, what, what you want, length and a rule against making things up. The AI still can't know details that matter, which is why the student edits.

### Prompt template (copy and paste)
```
I'm [who you are]. I need to [task]. Here's the situation: [2–3 real details]. Write [length and format]. Don't invent any facts; use [PLACEHOLDER] where you need information from me.
```

### Do it in 10 minutes
1. Choose one small task from your list.
2. Write a prompt using the template.
3. Read the result and change at least two things so it sounds like you.
4. Check every fact.

### Quiz
1. **Why did the second prompt give a better result?** It included real context, the goal, length and a rule against inventing details.
2. **What's the AI's role in this course?** A fast first-draft partner. You review and finish the work.
3. **Name the three checks before you send.** Did it invent anything? Does it sound like me? Is it accurate?
